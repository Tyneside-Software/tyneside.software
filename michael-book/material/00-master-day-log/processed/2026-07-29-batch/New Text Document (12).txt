Here’s the explanation:
1. The first quote is Matthew 15:14
“Let them alone; they are blind guides. And if the blind lead the blind, both will fall into a pit.”
This is Jesus speaking to his disciples. The Pharisees had just taken offense because Jesus taught that what defiles a person is not failing to follow their hand-washing traditions, but the evil that comes from the heart (Matthew 15:1–20). When the disciples point out that the Pharisees were offended, Jesus tells them to leave those religious leaders alone. He calls the Pharisees “blind guides” — people who claim spiritual authority yet lack true understanding. Anyone who follows them will share their fate and end up in spiritual disaster (“the pit”).
2. The second quote is John 15:14
“You are my friends if you do what I command.”
This comes from Jesus’ farewell teaching in the upper room (John 13–17), specifically the “true vine” section. After commanding his disciples to love one another as he has loved them, Jesus redefines their relationship with him: they are no longer merely servants but friends. The evidence (and condition) of that friendship is obedience to his commands.
Why “both/and”?
The two statements are completely different sayings of Jesus recorded in different Gospels. They happen to share the exact same chapter-and-verse reference (15:14). The tag “both/and” simply means the correct response is to identify both verses rather than choosing only one.synthesise both together into a single teachingSynthesised teaching from Matthew 15:14 and John 15:14:
Do not follow or waste energy on spiritually blind guides who claim religious authority yet lack true insight—leave them alone, for if the blind lead the blind, both will fall into a pit. Instead, become friends of Jesus by doing what He commands. True spiritual guidance, safety, and intimate relationship come only through obedient friendship with Christ Himself, not through human religious systems or leaders who cannot see the way.
How the two sayings fit together

Matthew 15:14 is a clear warning and instruction: when confronted with religious leaders (the Pharisees) who were offended by Jesus’ teaching that defilement comes from the heart rather than external traditions, Jesus tells His disciples to leave them alone. They are blind guides; following them leads both leader and follower into spiritual ruin.
John 15:14 is a positive invitation and definition of relationship: in the upper-room teaching about the true vine, Jesus says His disciples are His friends if they do what He commands (especially the command to love one another as He has loved them). Friendship with Him is marked by obedience.

Taken together, Jesus presents a sharp alternative: reject the path of blind human religious leadership that ends in the pit, and enter the path of true friendship with Him through obedient love. One leads to shared disaster; the other leads to intimate relationship with the One who sees clearly and calls His followers friends.
