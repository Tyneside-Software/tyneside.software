Tell me about
Priest in Charge: Rev'd Canon Dr Rae CaroRev'd Canon Dr Rae Caro is an Anglican priest in the Church of England’s Diocese of Newcastle. She serves as Priest in Charge of St Mark’s Church in Shiremoor, North Tyneside, and is a Non-Residentiary (Honorary) Canon of Newcastle Cathedral.
She took up the role of Priest in Charge at St Mark’s in June 2021. The church is described as friendly and inclusive in the Anglo-Catholic tradition. It serves Shiremoor and surrounding areas (including West Allotment, Murton, Moorside, Northumberland Park, Earsdon View and Cobalt Park). It has community links with groups such as the North Tyneside Disability Forum, Shiremoor Primary School, and local Guides and Scouting, runs a “Winged Lions” Sunday Club for children, a weekday lunch club, and other activities. Sunday Holy Communion is typically at 9:30 a.m.
Alongside her parish role, she has served as Mission Enabler for New Estates in Tynemouth Deanery, focusing on how churches can support communities amid new housing development through worship, toddler groups, coffee mornings, lunch clubs and other outreach. In a 2021 welcome visit by the then Mayor of North Tyneside, Norma Redfearn CBE, Caro spoke of a warm reception in Shiremoor and her hope of building energy and hope in the area after difficult times (including the pandemic).
In November 2024 the Bishop of Newcastle, the Rt Revd Dr Helen-Ann Hartley, appointed her (along with five others) as a Non-Residentiary/Honorary Canon of Newcastle Cathedral. She was installed at the cathedral’s Patronal Festival of St Nicholas on 6 December 2024. The College of Canons helps the cathedral support mission and ministry across the wider diocese.
Caro has publicly highlighted support for survivors of sexual violence as a significant part of her calling. In early 2022 she completed a 30-day running challenge (“Step out in Solidarity”) for Rape Crisis England and Wales during Sexual Abuse and Sexual Violence Awareness Week, running every day and covering 152.2 km (exceeding her 100 km goal) and raising £1,265. She noted that a year earlier she could not run 5 km.
Public information focuses mainly on her current and recent diocesan and parish roles rather than detailed personal biography, education, or earlier ministry.
