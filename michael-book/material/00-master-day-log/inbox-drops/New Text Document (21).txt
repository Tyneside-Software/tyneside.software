Finite energy → finite total agency
In any closed or resource-limited system (our universe, or a simulation running inside one), free energy is finite. Information processing has a thermodynamic cost (Landauer’s principle: erasing or overwriting a bit costs at least $  kT \ln 2  $ energy). Maintaining low-entropy internal models against thermal noise, predicting the future, and selecting actions all require continuous free-energy expenditure.
High-agency cognition is especially costly. A human brain is only ~2% of body mass yet consumes ~20% of the body’s energy budget. More sophisticated models, longer planning horizons, and greater originality demand even higher concentrations of free energy and compute. There is therefore an upper bound on the total amount of high-quality agency the system can support at any time.
Concentration is required
Because the total budget is finite, high agency cannot be distributed evenly. It must be concentrated.

Most of the system must run on low-cost, low-complexity scripts: simple feedback loops, pre-written behavioral trees, or highly compressed heuristics. These are the NPCs — entities whose behavior is predictable, locally optimized, and metabolically/computationally cheap.
A minority of nodes can receive enough free energy and complexity to maintain rich, flexible world-models and genuine choice. These are the high-agency characters (player characters, in game terms).

This is not a moral claim; it is a resource-allocation necessity. In a video-game engine with a fixed GPU/CPU budget, you do not give every background character full neural-net AI. You give them simplified state machines so the main characters can have depth. The same logic applies whether the “engine” is a physical universe or a simulation.
Scales of the pattern

Physics: Agency is the breaking of closed, reversible dynamics into irreversible choice. It converts low entropy into information (Rovelli and others). Most of the universe is near equilibrium or simple dynamical systems; concentrated free-energy gradients (stars, planets, brains) are rare.
Biology: Microbes and simple organisms run on tight metabolic budgets with limited behavioral repertoires. Complex nervous systems are expensive and therefore sparse.
Society / civilization: Influence, originality, and coordination capacity also follow heavy-tailed distributions. Most people follow existing scripts most of the time; a smaller set concentrates attention, capital, and decision rights.
Simulation: If we are in one, the same constraint is even sharper. High-fidelity agents are vastly more expensive to render and update than low-fidelity NPCs. Finite compute forces the designers (or the evolutionary process) to concentrate fidelity.

Expanding the total budget
The total amount of agency is not fixed forever. It scales with available free energy. Capturing more of the Sun’s output, moving to multiplanetary scales, or improving computational efficiency (useful operations per watt) expands the total agency the system can support. Even then, concentration remains necessary for peaks of agency; the background will still be dominated by lower-agency processes.
In short: finite energy levels impose a finite total capacity for high agency. Concentrated agency therefore requires concentrating energy (and the information-processing resources it enables). The rest of the system runs on cheaper scripts.
