What is the role of Gabriel?Archangel Gabriel (Hebrew: גַּבְרִיאֵל, Gaḇrîʾēl; also known as Jibrīl or Jibrāʾīl in Arabic) is primarily known in the Abrahamic religions—Judaism, Christianity, and Islam—as a high-ranking angel whose core role is that of a divine messenger who delivers God’s will, interprets visions, and announces major redemptive events.
The name means roughly “God is my strength,” “man of God,” or “God has shown himself mighty” (from Hebrew gever = man/strong one + ʾēl = God).
Key roles by tradition
Judaism
Gabriel appears in the Book of Daniel to interpret the prophet’s apocalyptic visions (Daniel 8 and 9), including the famous “seventy weeks” prophecy. In later Jewish tradition and texts such as 1 Enoch he is ranked among the chief angels, often associated with defending Israel and acting as one of the four principal guardian angels.
Christianity
In the New Testament (Gospel of Luke), Gabriel announces the birth of John the Baptist to Zechariah and, most famously, the conception of Jesus to the Virgin Mary (the Annunciation). Christian tradition therefore regards him as the angel of the Incarnation, of consolation, and of God’s power. He is venerated as a saint in Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Anglican, and Lutheran traditions (often commemorated with Michael and Raphael on 29 September in the Western calendar).
Islam
Known as Jibrīl, he is the angel of revelation. Muslims believe he transmitted the Qur’an to the Prophet Muhammad, beginning with the first verses of Surah Al-Alaq. He also appears to earlier prophets and is described as having an immense number of wings; he is associated with both communication of divine messages and, in some accounts, support in battle.
Across these traditions Gabriel consistently functions as the intermediary who brings God’s word to humanity—whether explaining prophetic mysteries, announcing the coming of key figures in salvation history, or delivering scripture itself.
