El name meaningEl primarily means “God” or “deity.”
Origin and Etymology
It comes from the Proto-Semitic root ʔil- (or Hebrew אֵל, ’el). In ancient Northwest Semitic languages it was both a common noun for any god and the proper name of a major deity.

Canaanite mythology: El (also spelled Il or Ilu) was the chief god—the father of the gods and of mankind, often called “Bull El” or “Father of Mankind.”
Hebrew Bible: The same word is used for the God of Israel (Yahweh). It appears hundreds of times, frequently in compound titles such as:
El Shaddai (“God Almighty”)
El Elyon (“God Most High”)
El Roi (“God Who Sees”)
El Olam (“Everlasting God”)


It also forms the theophoric element in many well-known biblical names:

Israel (“struggles with God”)
Michael (“who is like God?”)
Gabriel (“God is my strength”)
Samuel (“heard by God”)
Daniel, Elijah, Emmanuel, and dozens of others.

The root carries connotations of strength, power, and might.
As a Modern Given Name
“El” is rare as a standalone first name. When used today it is usually:

A short form of longer names such as Elijah, Elias, Eldridge (boys), or Eleanor / Elle / Ella (girls)
Occasionally chosen on its own for its short, strong sound and biblical resonance

In the United States it has never ranked in the top 1,000 and sees only a handful of uses most years. It appears more often as a nickname (for example, “El” for Eleven in Stranger Things) or in cultural references such as Superman’s Kryptonian family name “El” (Kal-El), which deliberately evokes the Hebrew meaning.
Other Notes

In Spanish, el is simply the masculine definite article (“the”), unrelated to the name’s Semitic origin.
Pronunciation is typically /ɛl/ (rhymes with “bell”).

In short, when people ask about the meaning of the name El, the answer that carries the most historical and cultural weight is the ancient Semitic word for God.
