## Outdated / captive-derived view

- From mid-20th-c. studies of **captive** unrelated wolves forced together.  
- Popularized partly via **L. David Mech 1970**; **does not** accurately describe typical **wild** packs.  
- **Alpha** pair: thought top dominants, control via aggression, exclusive breeding.  
- **Omega:** fixed lowest scapegoat / stress-absorber / sometimes jester-peacekeeper.  
- Still lives in media, dog-training myths, leadership analogies, fiction.

## Modern wild-pack understanding

- Packs usually **family units**: breeding pair (parents) + offspring; young disperse ~2–3 years.  
- “Alpha” **misleading** for natural packs — breeding male/female as **parents**, not dictators who fought up.  
- Mech later **corrected** terminology (1999 paper); urge retire “alpha wolf” language for wild animals.  
- Some researchers still say “alpha pair” casually for large/complex packs — still not captive ladder.  
- **Omega** fixed scapegoat role largely **captivity artifact**.
