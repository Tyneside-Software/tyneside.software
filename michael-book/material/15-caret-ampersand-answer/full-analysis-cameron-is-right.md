## 1. Is it a genuine (formal) paradox?

**No.**

A formal logical paradox (Liar, Russell, etc.) arises **inside a single formal system** from premises the system accepts, generating A ∧ ¬A.

The ^& construction does **not**:

- Dual readings require **switching frameworks**: math/text habit (^ as ASCII stand-in for ∧) vs programming (^ as XOR in nearly all languages; unary complement only in a minority such as Go).
- The string **^&** is not a native operator that simultaneously receives both truth-values. It is **juxtaposition**. In most languages: syntax error or sequential operators.
- The “both / not both” effect is **manufactured by the reader** combining selective interpretations after the fact.

**Verdict:** Cross-domain **polysemy** + selective reading + poetic synthesis. Apparent / literary paradox at best. **Not** structural formal paradox. Cameron’s core objection holds.
